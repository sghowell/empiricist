\title{Distance-hereditary graphs characterize minimum-fusion synthesis of graph states}

\author{Sean Howell}
\email{sean.g.howell@gmail.com}

\begin{abstract}
In fusion-based quantum computation a graph state $|G\rangle$ on $n$ qubits is assembled from
small entangled resource states by destructive two-qubit fusion measurements. We study the
minimum number $\F(G)$ of fusions when the resource is the three-qubit
Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger state, a fusion is a Bell-type measurement on any two qubits, and
single-qubit Clifford operations are free. We prove $\F(G)\ge n+3\,\rw(G)-6$ for every connected
graph $G$, where $\rw$ is the rank-width: the cycle rank of the network of resources and fusions
bounds the Schmidt rank of the output across every cut of a tree decomposition. At rank-width one
the bound is attained, so $\F(G)=n-3$ if and only if $G$ is distance-hereditary; the constructive
direction adds each pendant or twin of a distance-hereditary build with one fusion, and its
graph-theoretic core is a Lean 4 theorem. Both directions can be assembled from published results
on Schmidt-rank width and split decompositions, but we have not found the characterization stated.
An exhaustive search over all 585 connected local-complementation orbits on at most nine vertices,
with every construction accepted by two independent stabilizer engines, gives nineteen exact values
above the minimum: the rank-width bound is attained by all sixteen orbits with $\F=n$ and missed by
the three, all of rank-width two, that need $n+3$ fusions. The bound also raises the lower bound of
25 open orbits of rank-width three. The characterization was first proposed by a language model,
working inside a harness that admits only machine-checked artifacts, from labeled data on at most
seven vertices.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Fusion-based quantum computation (FBQC) builds fault-tolerant computation from a supply of small,
identical entangled \emph{resource states} that are joined by \emph{fusions}: destructive joint
measurements of two qubits~\cite{Bartolucci2023fusion}. The three-qubit
Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) state is the basic resource of photonic
proposals~\cite{GimenoSegovia2015three,Bartolucci2023fusion}, and the number of fusions is a main
physical cost, so for any target entangled state it is natural to ask how few fusions suffice to
make it. For graph states, the stabilizer states in one-to-one correspondence with simple
graphs~\cite{Hein2004multiparty,Hein2006entanglement}, this is a combinatorial optimization over
graphs that has been studied with heuristics and with dynamic programming in several resource
models~\cite{Lee2023graph,Lobl2025generating,Staudacher2026adaptive,Cashman2025finding,Ji2026towards}.

We work in the model whose resource is the three-qubit GHZ state, whose graph is the path $P_3$; each
fusion destructively measures a commuting pair of two-qubit Pauli operators that distinguishes the
four Bell states, and single-qubit Clifford operations are free. Let $\F(G)$ be the least number of
fusions that produce a state equal, up to single-qubit Cliffords and relabeling, to the graph state
$|G\rangle$. Counting qubits and connected components gives $\F(G)\ge n-3$ for a connected graph on
$n\ge 3$ vertices (Proposition~\ref{prop:count}). Our results are a lower bound in terms of rank-width
and the characterization of the graphs that attain the minimum.

\begin{theorem}[Rank-width bound]\label{thm:rw}
For every connected graph $G$ on $n\ge 3$ vertices, $\F(G)\ge n+3\,\rw(G)-6$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Characterization]\label{thm:main}
For a connected graph $G$ on $n\ge 3$ vertices, $\F(G)=n-3$ if and only if $G$ is
distance-hereditary.
\end{theorem}

Distance-hereditary graphs~\cite{Howorka1977characterization,Bandelt1986distance} are the graphs in
which every connected induced subgraph preserves distances. They are exactly the graphs of rank-width
at most one~\cite{Oum2005rank}, the graphs with no vertex-minor isomorphic to the
five-cycle~\cite{Bouchet1987isotropic,Bouchet1988transforming,Oum2017rank}, and the graphs obtained from
a single vertex by repeatedly adding pendant vertices and twins~\cite{Bandelt1986distance}. Rank-width
is invariant under local complementation~\cite{Oum2005rank} and equals the Schmidt-rank width of the
graph state~\cite{VandenNest2006universal,VandenNest2007classical}, so both results tie the fusion cost
to a local-Clifford-invariant measure of the entanglement structure of $|G\rangle$.

\paragraph{Relation to previous work.} Both theorems have close relatives. In the circuit model,
Kumabe, Mori and Yoshimura~\cite{Kumabe2026complexity} prove that preparing a connected graph state
needs at least $n+\rw(G)-2$ two-qubit Clifford gates, with exactly $n-1$ at rank-width one;
Theorem~\ref{thm:rw} is the analogue for fusions of three-qubit resources. In a model with
emitter-generated caterpillar resources, L\"obl \emph{et al.}~\cite{Lobl2025generating} tabulate exact
minimum fusion counts for all graph states on up to eight qubits and bound the count below by the
linear rank-width. The reverse direction of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} also follows from the equality of
Schmidt-rank width and rank-width~\cite{VandenNest2007classical}, and the forward direction from the
split-decomposition construction of distance-hereditary graph states by Connolly
\emph{et al.}~\cite{Connolly2026efficient} together with the construction of star graph states from
GHZ states by Lee and Jeong~\cite{Lee2023graph}. We have not found Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rw}, or a classification of minimum fusion counts in this model stated in the
literature (Section~\ref{sec:related}).

\paragraph{What is proved, and how.} The paper contains three kinds of argument.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Written proofs.} Theorem~\ref{thm:rw} and both directions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} are
  proved in Sections~\ref{sec:model} and~\ref{sec:main} from the graph rule for a fusion of two
  disjoint graph states (Lemma~\ref{lem:merge}) and standard facts about rank-width.
\item \emph{Lean theorems.} The graph-theoretic core of the constructive direction is checked by the
  Lean~4 kernel~\cite{deMoura2021lean} against \textsc{mathlib}~\cite{Mathlib2020lean} with only the
  axioms \code{propext}, \code{Classical.choice} and \code{Quot.sound}: a graph is reachable from $P_3$
  by $k$ pendant and false-twin steps together with free local complementations if and only if it
  belongs to the class generated from the connected three-vertex graphs by pendant and twin additions
  and local complementation, on $k+3$ vertices; this class is the distance-hereditary graphs. The
  development also contains a counting form of the lower bound and explicit constructions for paths,
  stars, trees, complete graphs and complete bipartite graphs (Section~\ref{sec:lean}).
\item \emph{Computation.} Two stabilizer engines that share no code agree with the fusion rule on
  random inputs, and an exhaustive search over all $585$ connected local-complementation orbits with
  $n\le 9$ agrees with Theorem~\ref{thm:main} on every orbit and gives exact values above the minimum
  (Section~\ref{sec:table}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{How the result was found.} The characterization was first proposed by a language model,
Fable~5, working inside a research harness that records nothing above the status of a heuristic
without a machine artifact and never lets the model execute code. The model classified the orbits,
proposed the distance-hereditary characterization from the $44$ labeled orbits with $n\le 7$, and wrote
the Lean proofs; the $541$ held-out orbits with $n\in\{8,9\}$ then agreed with the proposal without
exception (Section~\ref{sec:method}). Given the ingredients above, the characterization is within easy
reach of a specialist; we report its discovery as an instance of a harness producing a correct,
checkable conjecture from data, not as evidence that the result is hard.

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Graph states and local complementation.} For a simple graph $G=(V,E)$ the graph state
\[
  |G\rangle=\prod_{\{u,v\}\in E}\mathrm{CZ}_{uv}\,|{+}\rangle^{\otimes V}
\]
is the unique common $+1$ eigenvector of the operators $K_v=X_v\prod_{u\in\nbr(v)}Z_u$, $v\in V$,
where $\nbr(v)$ is the neighborhood of $v$. Local complementation at $v$ replaces the subgraph induced on $\nbr(v)$ by its
complement; we write $\tau_v(G)$. It is implemented on graph states by a product of single-qubit
Clifford unitaries~\cite{VandenNest2004graphical},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lc}
  |\tau_v(G)\rangle = e^{-i\pi X_v/4}\prod_{u\in\nbr(v)}e^{i\pi Z_u/4}\,|G\rangle,
\end{equation}
and two graph states on the same qubits are related by a product of single-qubit Clifford unitaries
if and only if their graphs are related by a sequence of local
complementations~\cite{VandenNest2004graphical}. The qubits that survive a schedule carry no fixed
labels, so we also identify isomorphic graphs, and write $G\sim_{\LC}H$ for the equivalence generated
by local complementation and isomorphism. For $A\subseteq V$ the \emph{cut-rank} $\rho_G(A)$ is the
rank over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ of the $A\times(V\setminus A)$ submatrix of the adjacency matrix, and the
Schmidt rank of $|G\rangle$ across $(A,V\setminus A)$ is $2^{\rho_G(A)}$~\cite{Hein2004multiparty}.

\paragraph{Resources, fusions and schedules.} A schedule starts from $g$ copies of the three-qubit GHZ
state; its graph is $P_3$, and it is $\LC$-equivalent to the triangle $K_3$. A \emph{fusion} on two
distinct qubits $a,b$ is the destructive measurement of the commuting observables $X_aZ_b$ and
$Z_aX_b$, after which $a$ and $b$ are discarded. This is a Bell measurement after a Hadamard on $b$,
the convention of Refs.~\cite{Lee2023graph,Lobl2025transforming}; any other Bell-type convention differs
from it by single-qubit Cliffords on $a$ and $b$, which are free in the model. A schedule interleaves
fusions, on any pairs of qubits, with arbitrary single-qubit Clifford gates. It \emph{produces} $G$ if,
for every outcome, the state of the surviving qubits is $\LC$-equivalent to $|G\rangle$; different
outcomes of a fusion on a stabilizer state differ by Pauli operators~\cite{Lee2023graph,Lobl2025transforming},
so this does not depend on the outcomes, and we treat fusions as deterministic, leaving the
probabilistic success of linear-optical fusion~\cite{Browne2005resource} to a layer above this problem.
The cost of a schedule is its number of fusions, and $\F(G)$ is the least cost of a schedule that
produces $G$.

\begin{lemma}[Fusion of disjoint graph states]\label{lem:merge}
Let $G_1$ and $G_2$ be graphs on disjoint vertex sets, $a$ a vertex of $G_1$ and $b$ a vertex of $G_2$.
A fusion on $(a,b)$ applied to $|G_1\rangle\otimes|G_2\rangle$ leaves, up to a Pauli operator that
depends on the outcomes, the graph state of the graph on $(V_1\cup V_2)\setminus\{a,b\}$ whose edges
are the edges of $G_1$ and $G_2$ not incident to $a$ or $b$, together with every pair $\{u,w\}$ with
$u\in\nbr_{G_1}(a)$ and $w\in\nbr_{G_2}(b)$.
\end{lemma}

This is the standard graph rule for type-II fusion~\cite{Browne2005resource}, stated in this form in
Refs.~\cite{Zaidi2015near,Lee2023graph} and in full generality in Ref.~\cite{Lobl2025transforming}. For
completeness: the observable $X_aZ_b$ anticommutes exactly with $K_b$ and with $K_u$ for $u\in\nbr(a)$,
and $Z_aX_b$ exactly with $K_a$ and with $K_w$ for $w\in\nbr(b)$. The standard update for measuring
$X_aZ_b$ and then $Z_aX_b$ replaces $K_u$ by $K_uK_b$ and $K_w$ by $K_wK_a$; multiplying these by
$Z_aX_b$ and $X_aZ_b$ respectively removes their support on $a$ and $b$ and leaves
$X_u Z_{\nbr(u)\setminus\{a\}}Z_{\nbr(b)}$ and $X_w Z_{\nbr(w)\setminus\{b\}}Z_{\nbr(a)}$ up to sign, the
stabilizers of the stated graph. Both of our stabilizer engines also reproduce the rule on random inputs
(Section~\ref{sec:method}).

Fusing a vertex $a$ of a graph with a qubit of a fresh resource therefore adds one vertex, in one of
three ways depending on the graph chosen to represent the resource (each is available, because
single-qubit Cliffords on a fresh resource are free). Fusing with a \emph{leaf} of $P_3$ replaces $a$ by
the center of $P_3$ and attaches the other leaf to it, which up to relabeling adds a \emph{pendant} at
$a$. Fusing with the \emph{center} of $P_3$ replaces $a$ by the two leaves, each with the neighborhood of
$a$ and not adjacent to each other, a \emph{false twin}. Fusing with a vertex of $K_3$ replaces $a$ by the
other two, adjacent, vertices, a \emph{true twin}. We call these fusions \emph{merges}.

\begin{proposition}[Counting bound]\label{prop:count}
Let a schedule with $g$ resources and $f$ fusions produce a connected graph on $n\ge 3$ vertices, and
let $H$ be the multigraph whose vertices are the resources, with one edge for each fusion joining the
resources that hold its two qubits. Then exactly one connected component $H_0$ of $H$ contains
surviving qubits; if it has $g_0$ resources, $f_0$ fusions and cycle rank $t=f_0-g_0+1$, then
$f\ge f_0=n-3+3t$. In particular $\F(G)\ge n-3$, and every schedule has $f\equiv n\pmod 3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fusions in different components of $H$ act on different tensor factors. If two components held
surviving qubits the output would be a product across them, which is impossible, since every cut-rank
of a connected graph is positive and so $|G\rangle$ is entangled across every bipartition. Counting the
qubits of $H_0$ gives $3g_0-2f_0=n$, and with $g_0=f_0-t+1$ this is $f_0=n-3+3t$; the cycle rank of a
connected multigraph is non-negative. The same count over all of $H$, whose other components have all
their qubits fused, gives $3g-2f=n$, so $f\equiv n\pmod 3$.
\end{proof}

A counting form of the bound, in a merge model of schedules that also tracks fusions which split or
consume a component, is the Lean theorem \code{fusion\_cost\_lower\_bound}.

\section{The rank-width bound and the characterization}\label{sec:main}

\paragraph{Rank-width.} A \emph{rank decomposition} of a graph $G$ on at least two vertices is a tree
$T$ whose internal nodes have degree three and whose leaves are the vertices of $G$. Each edge $e$ of
$T$ splits the leaves into two sides $(A_e,V\setminus A_e)$, and the width of $T$ is
$\max_e\rho_G(A_e)$. The rank-width $\rw(G)$ is the least width of a rank
decomposition~\cite{Oum2005rank,Oum2017rank}. A connected graph on at least two vertices has
$\rw(G)\ge 1$, and $\rw(G)\le 1$ if and only if $G$ is distance-hereditary~\cite{Oum2005rank}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rw}]
Take a schedule with $f=\F(G)$ fusions that produces $G$, and let $H_0$, $g_0$, $f_0$ and $t$ be as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:count}. A single-qubit gate commutes with every operation not acting on its qubit,
so we may apply each gate on a fused qubit to its resource before any fusion, and each gate on a
surviving qubit at the end, where it does not change Schmidt ranks. Fusions act on disjoint pairs of
qubits and commute. Conditioned on the outcomes, the state of the surviving qubits is then, up to
normalization and single-qubit unitaries,
\[
  \psi=\Big(\bigotimes_{e}\langle\beta_e|\Big)\Big(\bigotimes_{r}|R_r\rangle\Big),
\]
where $r$ runs over the resources of $H_0$, $|R_r\rangle$ is resource $r$ with its gates, $e$ runs over
the fusions of $H_0$, and $\langle\beta_e|$ is the two-qubit bra of the outcome of $e$; the components of
$H$ without survivors contribute only a scalar. Choose a spanning tree $S$ of $H_0$; then $t$ fusions of
$H_0$ lie outside $S$. Form a tree $T$ from $S$ by attaching each surviving qubit as a leaf to its
resource. A resource has three qubits, each surviving, fused along an edge of $S$, or fused along an
edge outside $S$, so its degree in $T$ is at most three. Repeatedly deleting resource nodes of degree
one and suppressing nodes of degree two turns $T$ into a rank decomposition $T'$ of $G$, after
identifying the surviving qubits with the vertices of $G$.

Each edge of $T'$ comes from a path in $T$, and all edges of that path induce the same cut of the
surviving qubits. If the path is a single leaf edge, the cut separates one vertex and has cut-rank at
most one. Otherwise it contains an edge $e$ of $S$; removing $e$ splits the resources of $H_0$ into two
sets $X$ and $Y$ and the surviving qubits into $A$ and $B$. The fusions joining $X$ and $Y$ are $e$ and
some of the $t$ fusions outside $S$. Contract all fusions inside $X$ and inside $Y$; each remaining bra
$\langle\beta|$ is a two-qubit state and so a sum of two product bras, and expanding them writes $\psi$
as a sum of at most $2^{1+t}$ products of a state on $A$ and a state on $B$. Hence the Schmidt rank of
$\psi$ across $(A,B)$ is at most $2^{1+t}$, and since $\psi$ is $\LC$-equivalent to $|G\rangle$,
$\rho_G(A)\le 1+t$. Every edge of $T'$ therefore has cut-rank at most $1+t$, so $\rw(G)\le 1+t$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:count} gives $\F(G)=f\ge n-3+3t\ge n-3+3(\rw(G)-1)$.
\end{proof}

The argument is the fusion analogue of bounding the Schmidt-rank width of a tensor network by its bond
structure~\cite{VandenNest2006universal,VandenNest2007classical}, and is close in spirit to the
linear-rank-width bound of Ref.~\cite{Lobl2025generating} and the gate bound of
Ref.~\cite{Kumabe2026complexity}. Only the cycle rank of the fusion network enters, so the bound holds
for any destructive two-qubit measurement with pure outcomes, not only for Bell-type fusions.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
($\Rightarrow$) If $\F(G)=n-3$, then $\rw(G)\le 1$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:rw}, so $G$ is
distance-hereditary~\cite{Oum2005rank}.

($\Leftarrow$) A connected distance-hereditary graph on $n\ge 3$ vertices is obtained from a connected
graph on three vertices, $P_3$ or $K_3$, by $n-3$ additions of pendant vertices, false twins and true
twins, every intermediate graph being connected~\cite{Bandelt1986distance}. Start from one resource
represented as $P_3$ or $K_3$, and realize each addition at a vertex $a$ by a merge with a fresh
resource, at a leaf of $P_3$, at the center of $P_3$ or at a vertex of $K_3$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:merge}
this schedule produces $G$ with $n-3$ fusions, and Proposition~\ref{prop:count} gives $\F(G)=n-3$.
\end{proof}

At the minimum the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rw} shows more: $t=0$, so the fusion network is a tree on
$n-2$ resources whose $n-3$ fusions each join two components. The constructive direction can also be
read off from the split decomposition. Every node of the split decomposition of a distance-hereditary
graph is a star or a complete graph~\cite{Bouchet1988transforming,Hammer1990completely}; Connolly
\emph{et al.}~\cite{Connolly2026efficient} assemble the graph state from the node graph states by
type-II fusions along the split tree, and Lee and Jeong~\cite{Lee2023graph} build an $m$-vertex star
from $m-2$ GHZ states with $m-3$ fusions. The totals are again $n-2$ resources and $n-3$ fusions.

\section{The Lean development}\label{sec:lean}

The Lean development is in the directory \code{lean/EmpiricistLean} of the accompanying repository,
against \textsc{mathlib} at release \code{v4.31.0}. The ancillary files contain the modules behind every
declaration we cite as a self-contained Lake project, with a script that builds it and prints the axioms
of each declaration (Table~\ref{tab:lean} in Appendix~\ref{app:lean}); all use only \code{propext},
\code{Classical.choice} and \code{Quot.sound}. The development formalizes the combinatorics of the
constructive direction. The qubit-level steps, Lemma~\ref{lem:merge} and the argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rw}, are not formalized.

\paragraph{Production predicates.} Graphs are \textsc{mathlib} \code{SimpleGraph}s on an arbitrary vertex
type, and the production predicates carry exact graphs rather than equivalence classes. Let
$\Prod^{+}_k(G)$ say that $G$ is obtained from $P_3$ by $k$ steps, each adding a pendant (the
\emph{leaf-merge}, \code{ghz3LeafMerge}) or a false twin (the \emph{center-merge}), interleaved with any
number of local complementations and isomorphisms (the inductive \code{ProducibleByExt}). The lemma
\code{ghz3CenterMerge\_iso\_addFalseTwin} identifies the center-merge, written as the graph rewrite of
Lemma~\ref{lem:merge}, with false-twin addition up to isomorphism. Let $\DHclass$ be the class generated
from $P_3$ and $K_3$ by pendant, false-twin and true-twin additions, local complementation and
isomorphism (the inductive \code{PendantTwinBuildable}). Without the local-complementation constructor
these are the connected distance-hereditary graphs on at least three vertices~\cite{Bandelt1986distance},
and the constructor adds nothing, since that class is closed under local
complementation~\cite{Oum2005rank}; this identification is not formalized.

\begin{theorem}[Model characterization, Lean]\label{thm:model}
For a finite graph $G$, $\Prod^{+}_{|V(G)|-3}(G)$ holds if and only if $G\in\DHclass$
(\code{dh\_characterization}).
\end{theorem}

The forward implication is a structural induction on $\Prod^{+}$: each constructor of $\Prod^{+}$ is one
of $\DHclass$. The converse (\code{dh\_forward}) is a structural induction on $\DHclass$: a pendant
addition is a leaf-merge, a false-twin addition a center-merge, and a true-twin addition is realized by
local complementation, a leaf-merge and local complementation, through the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truetwin}
  \tau_u\big(\mathrm{addPendant}(G,u)\big)\;=\;\mathrm{addTrueTwin}\big(\tau_u(G),\,u\big)
\end{equation}
(\code{addPendant\_localComplement\_center}) and the involutivity of $\tau_u$. With
\code{fusion\_cost\_lower\_bound}, \code{dh\_min\_fusions} states the constructive direction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} at the level of the model. A further declaration, \code{floor\_schedule\_iff\_dh},
extends Theorem~\ref{thm:model} to a schedule model with one more constructor, which may replace the
current graph by \emph{any} graph with two fewer vertices at the cost of one fusion, an
over-approximation of a fusion of two qubits of one component; at the minimum a vertex count excludes
that constructor.

\paragraph{Families.} Before the general theorem, the development proves constructions at the minimum
for natural families, each stated together with the general lower bound so that no family theorem can
be true for a vacuous reason: paths, stars and all trees by leaf-removal induction, $K_n$ by one local
complementation from a star, and $K_{m,n}$ by three local complementations from a double star. All of
these graphs are distance-hereditary.

\begin{remark}[Induction must track concrete graphs]\label{rem:concrete}
Although every statement about $\F$ is invariant under local complementation, the inductions cannot be
run on $\sim_{\LC}$ classes, because pendant addition does not descend to them: $P_3\sim_{\LC}K_3$, yet a
pendant at the center of $P_3$ gives the four-vertex star, while a pendant at a vertex of $K_3$ gives the
paw, which is $\LC$-equivalent to the path $P_4$ and not to the star. The development therefore carries
exact graphs through every induction and applies local complementation only explicitly. The first
production model had only the leaf-merge and so generated only trees; it witnesses the minimum only on
the local-complementation classes of trees, which are $93$ of the $175$ distance-hereditary orbits with
$n\le 9$, one for each tree on $3$ to $9$ vertices, since locally equivalent trees are
isomorphic~\cite{Bouchet1988transforming}. Adding the center-merge removed the obstruction.
\end{remark}

\section{The certified tablebase}\label{sec:table}

\paragraph{Search.} Every connected graph with $3\le n\le 9$ was assigned to its local-complementation
orbit; there are $1, 2, 4, 11, 26, 101, 440$ orbits for $n=3,\dots,9$, $585$ in all, in agreement with
Danielsen and Parker~\cite{Danielsen2006classification}. By Proposition~\ref{prop:count} the search
proceeds in tiers $\F=n-3$, $\F=n$, and so on. Tier~0 is a breadth-first search over graphs in which
local complementation is free and a merge at any vertex, with a fresh resource fused at a leaf or at
the center, costs one fusion; true twins arise through~\eqref{eq:truetwin}. Tier~1 grows graphs by
merges to $n+2$ vertices and then performs one fusion of two qubits of the component. Both are
exhaustive by the normal form of Appendix~\ref{app:normal}: any schedule can be reordered so that merges
come first and grow a single component, and single-qubit Cliffords on the qubits of a merge are
absorbed by local complementations. Every fusion in the search is computed in the fixed basis of
Section~\ref{sec:model}. For the one fusion inside the component in Tier~1 we re-ran the search with each
of the six classes of single-qubit Clifford on one fused qubit, which together with the symmetry of the
measured pair covers all choices on both qubits (for $n\le 5$ all $36$ pairs were tried), and no orbit
changed. Each recorded construction is replayed by two stabilizer engines that share no code, a tableau
simulator built on \textsc{stim}~\cite{Gidney2021stim} and an independent implementation over
$\mathrm{GF}(2)$, with canonical forms from \textsc{nauty}~\cite{McKay2014practical}. The table, with
$585$ rows each holding an orbit representative and either an exact value with a witness schedule or a
lower bound, is a single content-addressed artifact; nine exact values above the minimum were added
later from model-proposed constructions.

with $n\in\{8,9\}$ of rank-width three, whose bound $\F\ge n+3$ comes from Theorem~\ref{thm:rw}; the two
open orbits with $n=7$ have $\F\ge n+3$ from the exhaustion of Tier~1.}
\label{tab:counts}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:counts} summarizes the table. A pendant-and-twin elimination
check~\cite{Bandelt1986distance}, run independently on all $585$ representatives, confirms that the
orbits with $\F=n-3$ are exactly the distance-hereditary ones. Nineteen orbits have exact values above
the minimum. The sixteen at $\F=n$ have upper bounds from constructions and lower bounds from
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The three at $\F=n+3$ are a six-vertex orbit at $\F=9$, realized with eight
resources, nine fusions and three local complementations, and two seven-vertex orbits at $\F=10$; their
lower bounds come from the exhaustion of Tier~1.

\paragraph{How tight is the rank-width bound?} We computed the rank-width of every orbit above the
minimum exactly, by enumerating all rank decompositions. Every orbit with $n\le 7$ that is not
distance-hereditary has rank-width two, and so do all sixteen orbits with $\F=n$, which therefore attain
the bound $\F\ge n$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:rw}. The three orbits with $\F=n+3$ also have rank-width two, so
the bound misses them by three: above rank-width one the fusion cost is not a function of rank-width. In
the other direction the bound improves the table. Twenty-five open orbits, one with $n=8$ and $24$ with
$n=9$, have rank-width three, so $\F\ge n+3$, where the search had established only $\F\ge n$.

\section{Method: the harness and the discovery}\label{sec:method}

The results were obtained with Empiricist, an open-source harness that drives a language model through
research tasks under an explicit discipline. Its source, the Lean development, the tablebase and the
ledger of claims are in the accompanying repository.

\paragraph{A ledger of claims with evidence.} Every artifact (a computed value, a construction, a
conjecture, a Lean module) is recorded at a status in the ordered set \textsc{refuted} $<$
\textsc{heuristic} $<$ \textsc{conjectured} $<$ \textsc{verified}$_n$ $<$ \textsc{certified} $<$
\textsc{formalized} and rises only with an evidence record from a certified verifier; \textsc{refuted} is
terminal, and nothing the model merely asserts is recorded above \textsc{heuristic}. Artifacts are
addressed by content hash, and verifiers by the hash of their code and of a golden test suite that they
must pass before use.

\paragraph{The model never executes.} The model emits structured output, such as a schedule, a conjecture
or a complete Lean module, and the harness runs every check in a sandbox.

\paragraph{Two stabilizer engines.} Fusion outcomes are computed by two engines that share no code, and a
construction is accepted only when both agree on the resulting state up to local Clifford equivalence.
The closed-form merge rule used in the hot loop of the search matches both engines on randomized inputs.

\paragraph{A hardened Lean gate.} A submitted Lean module is elaborated in a sandbox without network or
process creation, against a frozen read-only \textsc{mathlib}; the kernel result is re-checked, the
axioms must lie within $\{\code{propext},\code{Classical.choice},\code{Quot.sound}\}$, every import must
belong to an explicit trusted set, and the build directory is swept after elaboration. The gate was
developed against adversarial submissions (poisoned object files, swaps between check and use,
axiom-hiding elaborators) before it was used.

\paragraph{The discovery.} The harness gave the model the labeled catalogue of the $44$ orbits with
$n\le 7$, each as an edge list tagged extremal or not, with no invariant, class name or hint beyond the
true statement that the property is invariant under local complementation. The model proposed that the
extremal orbits are the distance-hereditary graphs, gave the standard equivalent characterizations, and
identified the smallest obstruction: the unique non-extremal orbit on five vertices is the orbit of
$C_5$. The proposal entered the ledger as a conjecture, and a deterministic distance-hereditary check on
all $585$ orbits, including the $541$ on eight and nine vertices that the model had not seen, agreed with
it without exception. The model then wrote the Lean proofs through a loop in which the gate returns the
remaining proof obligations and the model revises; it never ran Lean itself. The discipline also caught
errors along the way: an arithmetic slip in an early hand-written note of the extremal split
($185/400$ instead of $175/410$), a family theorem whose first statement had the construction built
into the claim, and, in preparing this paper, a note that reported the linear rank-width of the
six-vertex orbit with $\F=9$ as its rank-width.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Fusion cost.} Lee and Jeong~\cite{Lee2023graph} optimize fusion-based generation of graph
states from GHZ states with graph-theoretical heuristics, construct stars with the minimum number of
fusions, and state the graph rule for fusion used here. L\"obl \emph{et al.}~\cite{Lobl2025generating}
compute, by dynamic programming over local-complementation orbits, exact minimum fusion counts for all
graph states on up to eight qubits when the inputs are caterpillar states from a single quantum emitter;
their zero-fusion class is the local-complementation classes of caterpillars, and they bound the count
below by the linear rank-width. Their model differs from ours in the resource, so the values and the
extremal class differ; the term tablebase is theirs. Staudacher \emph{et al.}~\cite{Staudacher2026adaptive}
and Cashman \emph{et al.}~\cite{Cashman2025finding} optimize fusion networks with linear-cluster
resources, and Ji \emph{et al.}~\cite{Ji2026towards} decompose targets into ring, star and linear motifs
joined by type-I fusion.

\paragraph{Preparation cost in other models.} Kumabe, Mori and Yoshimura~\cite{Kumabe2026complexity}
determine the number of two-qubit Clifford gates needed to prepare a graph state up to rank-width terms;
their lower bound $n+\rw(G)-2$ and their exact value $n-1$ at rank-width one are the circuit analogues of
Theorems~\ref{thm:rw} and~\ref{thm:main}. Davies and Jena~\cite{Davies2025preparing} bound the
preparation cost of graph states forbidding a vertex-minor. Minimum-gate and minimum-edge
representatives of local-complementation orbits were studied by Cabello
\emph{et al.}~\cite{Cabello2011optimal} and by Sharma \emph{et al.}~\cite{Sharma2026minimising}.

\paragraph{Distance-hereditary graph states.} Connolly \emph{et al.}~\cite{Connolly2026efficient}
construct every distance-hereditary graph state by fusions along its split decomposition, and Connolly,
Nishio and Nemoto~\cite{Connolly2026local} count the local-equivalence classes of families of
distance-hereditary graphs. The orbits of graph states were classified by Hein
\emph{et al.}~\cite{Hein2004multiparty} for $n\le 7$ and by Danielsen and
Parker~\cite{Danielsen2006classification} for $n\le 12$, and mapped by Adcock
\emph{et al.}~\cite{Adcock2020mapping}. Transformation problems for graph states under local
operations, efficient at bounded rank-width and hard in general, were studied by Dahlberg, Helsen and
Wehner~\cite{Dahlberg2020how,Dahlberg2020transforming,Dahlberg2020counting}.

\paragraph{Machine-assisted mathematics.} Language models have recently produced new constructions in
combinatorics and analysis through program
search~\cite{RomeraParedes2024mathematical,Novikov2025alphaevolve,Georgiev2025mathematical}, and formal
proof has been combined with machine-generated mathematics~\cite{Trinh2024solving,Hubert2026olympiad}.
Here a model proposes a characterization from data and writes the Lean proofs, and a harness admits only
what a verifier accepts.

\section{Discussion and open questions}\label{sec:discussion}

The combinatorics of the constructive direction is checked in Lean; the qubit-level steps are written
proofs, corroborated by two stabilizer engines and by the exhaustive search. A first-principles
formalization would define graph states, Pauli measurements and schedules in Lean and derive
Lemma~\ref{lem:merge} and the argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:rw}; rank-width is not yet in
\textsc{mathlib}. Three questions remain.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A finer invariant.} Above rank-width one, $\F$ is not a function of rank-width
  (Section~\ref{sec:table}). Which invariant decides between $\F=n$ and $\F=n+3$ at rank-width two?
\item \emph{Complexity.} Theorem~\ref{thm:main} makes the decision problem $\F(G)=n-3$ polynomial, since
  distance-hereditary graphs are recognized by pendant-and-twin elimination~\cite{Bandelt1986distance}.
  The complexity of computing $\F$ in general is open.
\item \emph{Other resources.} Do analogous characterizations hold for larger GHZ states or for
  linear-cluster resources?
\end{enumerate}

\section{The normal form behind the search}\label{app:normal}

The exhaustiveness of Tiers~0 and~1 rests on two lemmas.

\begin{lemma}[Absorbing single-qubit Cliffords]\label{lem:frame}
Let $v$ be a vertex of a graph $G$ with at least one neighbor, and let $C$ be a single-qubit Clifford
unitary. Then there are a graph $G'$, obtained from $G$ by local complementations, and a product $D$ of
single-qubit Cliffords acting trivially on $v$, such that $C_v|G\rangle=D\,|G'\rangle$ up to a Pauli
operator and a global phase.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Single-qubit Clifford unitaries modulo Pauli operators and phases form a group isomorphic to the
symmetric group on $\{X,Y,Z\}$, acting by conjugation, and $e^{-i\pi X/4}$ and $e^{i\pi Z/4}$ act as the
transpositions $(Y\,Z)$ and $(X\,Y)$, which generate it. By~\eqref{eq:lc}, local complementation at $v$
multiplies the local unitary on $v$ by $e^{-i\pi X_v/4}$, and local complementation at a neighbor $u$ of
$v$ multiplies it by $e^{i\pi Z_v/4}$. Neither removes the edge $\{u,v\}$, since $\tau_v$ changes only
edges inside $\nbr(v)$ and $\tau_u$ only edges inside $\nbr(u)$. So a suitable word in $\tau_v$ and
$\tau_u$ gives a graph $G'$ and a product $U=\bigotimes_w U_w$ of single-qubit Cliffords with
$U|G\rangle=|G'\rangle$ up to a Pauli operator and $U_v=C$ modulo Paulis. Then
$C_v|G\rangle=\big(\bigotimes_{w\neq v}U_w^{-1}\big)U|G\rangle$, which is $D|G'\rangle$ with
$D=\bigotimes_{w\ne v}U_w^{-1}$, up to a Pauli operator.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Single-component normal form]\label{lem:normal}
Suppose a schedule with $f$ fusions produces a connected graph $G$ on $n$ vertices. Then some schedule
with $f'\le f$ fusions and $g'$ resources, $3g'-2f'=n$, produces $G$ and has the following form. One
resource is the seed. Each of the other $g'-1$ resources is attached by a merge, a fusion between a qubit
of the current component, which is a connected graph state, and a qubit of the fresh resource, with no
single-qubit Clifford on either fused qubit; local complementations of the component may occur between
merges. After all merges come the remaining $f'-g'+1$ fusions, each between two qubits of the component,
and single-qubit Cliffords on the surviving qubits come last.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Single-qubit Cliffords.} A single-qubit Clifford on a qubit $q$ commutes with every operation that
does not act on $q$, and the only other operation acting on $q$ is the fusion that consumes it, if any.
Move each such gate to just after the preparation of its resource, to just before the fusion that
consumes $q$, or to the end of the schedule if $q$ survives.

\emph{Discard what does not reach the output.} Delete the components of the multigraph $H$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:count} that contain no surviving qubit; they act on separate tensor factors. Then $H$
is connected, with $g'$ resources and $f'\le f$ fusions, and $3g'-2f'=n$.

\emph{Reorder.} Choose a spanning tree of $H$, a root resource, and a breadth-first order of the resources
from the root along the tree. For each non-root resource $c$ with parent $p$, pick one fusion joining $p$
and $c$; these are the merges. Put the merges first, in the order of their child resources, followed by all
other fusions in their original relative order. Fusions act on disjoint pairs of qubits and commute, so the
final state on the surviving qubits, conditioned on the outcomes, does not change; only the Pauli
corrections may. When the merge of $c$ is performed, its qubit in $p$ has not been consumed and $c$ has not
yet been touched, so it fuses a qubit of the component grown so far with a qubit of a fresh resource; every
later fusion acts on two qubits of that component.

\emph{Remove the Cliffords on merge qubits.} Process the merges in order. Before the merge of $c$ the
component is the graph state of a connected graph $B$ on at least three vertices, up to single-qubit
Cliffords: the seed has three vertices, and each merge keeps the graph connected by
Lemma~\ref{lem:merge}, since both joined neighborhoods are non-empty, and adds one vertex. Its fused qubit
$a$ has a neighbor, so Lemma~\ref{lem:frame} replaces the Clifford on $a$ by local complementations of $B$
and Cliffords on other qubits, which are carried forward. On the fresh side, Lemma~\ref{lem:frame} applied
to the three-vertex graph of the resource removes the Clifford on its fused qubit, leaving $P_3$ with some
center or $K_3$. The merge is then a fusion without single-qubit Cliffords on either fused qubit.
Cliffords carried onto qubits consumed by later merges are removed in the same way when those merges are
reached; Cliffords on qubits consumed by the final fusions inside the component stay, as the statement
allows; the rest move to the end.
\end{proof}

At the minimum the normal form has no fusion inside the component, so Tier~0 is exhaustive for $\F=n-3$.
Tier~1, which applies one such fusion after all merges, is exhaustive for $\F=n$, given the re-run over the
single-qubit Cliffords on its two fused qubits described in Section~\ref{sec:table}.
